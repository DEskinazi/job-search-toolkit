# Application Log

Tracks in-progress applications with rate and intangibles for comparison. This file is for
*evaluating and comparing opportunities* — it is not where your career facts live. See
`career_context_template.md` for that; read its own setup instructions first if you haven't.

**Why two separate files:** career facts stay true regardless of what job you're looking at, and
change slowly. Opportunity evaluations are specific to a single posting, change fast (a posting
closes, a recruiter replies, pay gets disclosed), and there are many of them at once. Mixing the
two makes both harder to trust — you end up unsure whether a "fact" in your resume file is
actually a note about one specific application. Keep them apart.

Fill in every `[bracketed]` section. Delete this instructions block once you've read it.

### More constraint categories worth deciding explicitly

Decide each of these before you evaluate a single posting. Write "none" if one does not apply
to you. A constraint left blank gets applied inconsistently, usually in whichever direction
feels best at 11pm.

- **Pay floor:** [your minimum base salary, and how to read a posted range. For example: judge a
  role by the top of its range, not the midpoint, or by base pay only. Say what to do when pay
  is not posted.]
- **Pay tiers:** [if you want roles ranked, say how: for example, tier 1 at or above [number],
  tier 2 between [number] and [number]. Say how contract rates convert to a yearly figure.]
- **Role type:** [individual contributor, manager, or both. If you would take a well-paid
  individual-contributor role over a lower-paid management one, say so, so the agent does not
  filter on the word "manager."]
- **Remote requirement:** [fully remote only, remote or hybrid, any. State whether this is a
  hard filter or a preference.]
- **Commute limit:** [for anything not fully remote, the farthest you will go, written as places
  (name the cities that are in range and the first ones that are not), not as a vague radius.]
- **Office-day cap:** [the most days a week you will be on site. Decide how to treat a posting
  that does not state its schedule. A good default is to label it "cadence unconfirmed" and
  report it, not to pass or reject it silently.]
- **Health, accessibility or caregiving constraints:** [optional. If you have a real need that
  limits commuting, schedule or travel, write it as a hard filter with one line on why, so the
  agent treats it as non-negotiable. You do not need to share details, and you should keep
  anything private out of a public copy of this file.]
- **Schedule, time zone and travel:** [working hours you need, time zones you can cover, and
  the amount of travel you accept.]
- **Authorization and sponsorship:** [work authorization, relocation, or visa requirements that
  rule roles in or out.]
- **Process authority:** [do you want license to design how the work is done, or are you fine
  executing a defined process? See `body_text_signals_template.md`.]
- **Work you enjoy:** [the specific activities you want more of. Ask the agent to note which of
  them each role offers, so you can compare roles on more than title and pay.]

## OPPORTUNITY EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

This is the reusable part — copy this section as-is into your own log, it doesn't depend on your
specific career facts. It gives an LLM (or you, at a glance) a consistent way to sort every
posting into a tier, instead of re-deciding the criteria fresh for every single one.

**Hard filters (fail either → automatic PASS, no exceptions, regardless of timeline pressure):**
1. [Your first true non-negotiable — location, remote status, an accommodation need, etc.]
2. [Your second true non-negotiable — usually a pay floor below which the role doesn't solve
   the actual problem you're trying to solve, regardless of how appealing it is otherwise]

**GOOD opportunity:** Scope is a genuine match to your real experience. Title is in your
calibration zone. Pay is disclosed and reasonable, or the company is large/stable enough that
undisclosed pay isn't itself a red flag. No red-flag culture language in the posting itself.

**STRETCH opportunity:** A real reach on title, scope, or a specific named qualifications gap —
but one you can honestly address rather than gloss over (see the gap-scrutiny section in
`career_context_template.md`). Worth applying, but go in knowing exactly what the stretch is.

**PASS:** Culture red flags in the posting's own language (name your own trigger phrases here
as you notice them — e.g., "the bar is high," "not for the faint of heart," anything that reads
as normalizing overwork). Pay too low even adjusted for the role type. A hard filter fails with
no exception. Real scope is unclear or IC/advisory-only when the title implied otherwise.

## WHEN TO LOWER THE BAR: TIMELINE VS. HOLDING OUT

Define your own phases based on your actual runway. The point of naming phases explicitly is so
that "I should probably be less picky now" is a decision you make on purpose, on a known
timeline, rather than something that creeps up on you unconsciously as pressure builds.

**What never flexes, regardless of time pressure:** your hard filters, defined above.

**What flexes as time compresses:** title, scope-perfection, culture nice-to-haves — NOT the
hard filters.

- **Phase 1 ([dates] — full leverage):** Apply broadly across GOOD and STRETCH tiers only.
- **Phase 2 ([dates] — the window in which an offer realistically needs to land):** If GOOD-tier
  hasn't converted to offers, escalate STRETCH-tier engagement and reconsider PASS-tier roles
  rejected only for *soft* reasons — not the hard filters.
- **Phase 3 (approaching [your real financial cliff — e.g., benefits lapsing] and beyond):**
  Previously-PASS roles on pay (if still above your true survival floor) and previously-STRETCH
  roles regardless of qualification gaps become reasonable to actively pursue, not just apply to
  opportunistically.

## A NOTE ON VERIFICATION

Job boards (LinkedIn especially, when fetched without being logged in) sometimes show
placeholder or stale location/pay data instead of the real listing. Before treating any
location, pay, or "still open" claim as fact, verify it against the employer's own careers page
or applicant-tracking system directly, not just the aggregator that surfaced it. This matters
most for anything touching a hard filter — a wrong "Remote" tag is the kind of error that costs
real time.
